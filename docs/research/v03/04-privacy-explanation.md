# 04 — Privacy explanation

Issue #54. Sent to every candidate after they pass the screener and before the interview is held.
Written to be read by the participant, not by the project.

Send it as-is. If a participant asks a question this document does not answer, answer honestly and

## Participant-facing text

### What this study is

I'm doing research on how people handle commitments that slip — the thing you meant to do and
didn't. I interview 30 to 40 adults, write down what happened in a structured form, and count
patterns across everyone. Your interview becomes a handful of yes/no codes in a spreadsheet.

I'm not a clinician. This is not a medical, psychological, or diagnostic assessment, and nothing in
it is advice or treatment.

### What I record

- Structured codes: whether you described a specific past event, whether it recurs weekly, whether
  it had a concrete cost, which tools you use, which you've abandoned, whether you've paid for
  anything similar, how hard switching tools felt, and what you'd refuse to share with software.
- A short private note in my own words describing what happened, so that a second person can check
  my coding.
- The date of the interview and which of the two participant groups you're in.

### What I do not record

- Your name, email address, phone number, home address, or employer, anywhere near your answers.
- Verbatim transcripts or quotes.
- Any diagnosis, medication, treatment, or health information. If you mention one, I won't write it
  down.
- Names of other people you talk about. If you say "my sister", I write "a family member".
- Anything from your calendar, messages, email, or apps. I don't connect to anything.

### How your identity is separated from your answers

Your answers are filed under a code like `int-014`. The list matching codes to people is a separate
file with separate access, and it is the only place your contact details exist. The two are never
sent together, never published, and never put into the project's source code repository.

What eventually becomes public is arithmetic: "of 32 people interviewed, N reported X". Nothing that
could identify you leaves the study.

### No recording

I don't record audio or video, at all. I type structured notes while we talk, so expect a few
pauses while I catch up.

### Payment

You get **75 shekels** for completing the interview, about 45 minutes. It's the same for everyone.
It doesn't depend on your answers, on whether you turn out to fit what the study is looking for, or
on saying anything positive about anything. If you stop partway through, I still pay you.

### Who sees it

Only me, and — for a subset of interviews checked for coding accuracy — one other person, who sees
the coded notes without your contact details. No third party, no advertiser, no model provider.
Nothing is used to train a model.

### How long it's kept

This is my working policy for this study. It's how I intend to handle your data; it isn't a legal
statement and I'm not claiming it satisfies any particular regulation.

- **Your contact details, and the list linking codes to people:** deleted 30 days after I finish
  recruiting — unless you separately told me I may contact you in future, in which case I keep only
  what's needed for that.
- **Interview notes containing anything identifying:** turned into codes, then the identifiable
  version is deleted, at the latest 30 days after the project review this research feeds into.
- **The coded, de-identified answers:** kept until a later market review, plus 90 days. By then
  they're counts and codes with nothing traceable to you.

### Your rights

- Skip any question, without giving a reason.
- Stop the interview at any moment, and still be paid.
- Withdraw afterwards. I delete your notes and your entry in the code list, and your interview
  leaves the study entirely — it isn't counted as a negative answer. You can do this up until the
  data has been de-identified and locked for the combined analysis; after that there's genuinely
  nothing left that points at you, so there's nothing I could pull out. I'll warn you before that
  point arrives.
- Ask me what I have recorded about you, and I'll show you your row.

To do any of these, email me at **[STUDY_CONTACT_EMAIL]**. You don't need to explain.

### The separate question about the pilot

At the end of the interview I'll ask whether I may contact you later about a small closed test of an
early tool. That's a separate permission. Saying no changes nothing about your interview, and saying
yes doesn't commit you to anything — it means I may send you one message.

If you do join that pilot later, it has its own consent, its own controls, and its own explanation.
You'd be able to see everything it holds, mute it, revoke it, or delete it, and it never reads your
messages or your calendar unless you specifically switch that on.

### Questions

Ask me anything at [STUDY_CONTACT_EMAIL], before or after the interview.

## Internal notes — not sent to participants

- **This document contains no unresolved participant-facing placeholders.** Compensation, recording,
  retention, withdrawal, and the contact address are all settled. Sending a version of this document
  with a placeholder in it is a consent defect; if you ever reintroduce one, do not send it.
- The retention wording deliberately says "my working policy for this study" and makes no claim
  about legal compliance. Do not add one, in any language version, even if asked.
- The withdrawal window closes when the dataset is de-identified and locked for aggregate analysis.
  Warn participants before that point rather than letting it pass silently, and never imply deletion
  is still possible afterwards.
- The claims here are enforced mechanically, not just promised: `lib/research/v03BehavioralResearch.ts`
  rejects any coded record containing a name, email, phone, address, transcript, raw text, or
  diagnosis field, and `lib/research/v03FieldIntake.ts` rejects any tracker cell that looks like a
  direct identifier. If a claim in this document ever stops being enforced by those two files, the
  document is wrong and must be corrected before the next interview.
- The compensation log lives with the identity map, never in a tracker. Paying someone is a fact
  about a person, not about a coded interview.
