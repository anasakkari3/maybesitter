# 02 — Recruitment messages

Issue #54. Every outbound message in the study. Use them as written; edits are how medical framing,
product promises, and leading language creep back in.

`[SQUARE BRACKETS]` are the only things you fill in before sending. `[STUDY_CONTACT_EMAIL]`
comes from the private study contact sheet and is intentionally not stored in Git; R4 and R7 carry
the remaining message-specific placeholders. Compensation (75 ILS, unconditional) is settled — see
the approved decisions in [README.md](README.md).

**Campus channels are blocked.** `CAMPUS_ETHICS_STATUS = unresolved`: R3 must not be posted on a
university or college noticeboard, faculty list, or official student channel until Anas determines
whether ethics approval or notification is required. R1, R2, and non-institutional routes are
unaffected.

**Never in any message:** "AI assistant", "ADHD app", "would you use", "do you like", "revolutionary",
"help you finally get organised", any diagnosis word, any therapeutic or medical claim, any promise
of early access in exchange for participating.

**Never frame the 75 ILS as conditional.** Not on qualifying, not on the answers, not on joining the
pilot, not on liking anything. "Paid if you're a fit" is a different study with a different — and
worse — response distribution, because it tells candidates which answers get them paid.

The Arabic and Hebrew versions below are for the bilingual student cohort. Have a native speaker
read them once before the first post — they are written to be plain and correct, but a recruitment
message that reads as translated depresses response rates.

---

## R1 — Commercial cohort, community post

> **Looking for people who miss things that matter — 45-minute research interview**
>
> I'm doing research on how adults handle commitments that slip through: the thing you meant to do,
> knew you had to do, and still didn't start.
>
> I'm looking for people 18+ who deal with this regularly and who have paid for something to help —
> an app, a subscription, a coach, a course, a planner — whether or not it worked.
>
> The interview is 45 minutes and is entirely about what actually happened: the last time something
> slipped, what you did next, which tools you've abandoned and why. There is no product to try, no
> demo, and nothing to buy. I'm not asking anything medical and I'm not collecting diagnoses.
>
> My notes are stored under a code instead of your name, and your contact details are never kept
> with your answers. You can stop at any point.
>
> Completing the interview is paid: 75 ILS, the same for everyone, regardless of your answers
> or of whether you turn out to fit the study.
>
> If you're interested, reply here or message me at [STUDY_CONTACT_EMAIL] and I'll send five short screening
> questions.

## R5 — Single follow-up after no reply

Send once, after 3 days. Never a second time.

> Hi — following up once on the research interview. If it's not a fit or the timing is bad, no reply
> needed and I won't chase it. If you'd still like to take part, just say when suits you.

## R6 — Not qualified

> Thanks for taking the time to answer. This particular study needs a fairly narrow group and it's
> not a fit this time, so I won't take up your 45 minutes. I appreciate you replying.

Do not say which criterion they missed. Do not offer to keep them on a list unless you have a list
and a lawful basis for it.

## R7 — Closed-pilot invitation

**Not sendable during #54.** The pilot belongs to #55 and its exposure is not authorised. Its terms
— duration, time commitment, and any separate compensation — are set by #55, not by this kit, which
is why the bracket below stays open. This message is drafted here only so that the handoff has
somewhere to land.

When it is eventually sent, it goes **only** to candidates who passed every rule in
[13-pilot-handoff-rules.md](13-pilot-handoff-rules.md), and only after their interview is coded.
Sending this before coding is the most likely way to put an unqualified person into the cohort.

> Hi — when we spoke you said I could contact you separately about a small closed test. This is that.
>
> It's a limited pilot with 25–40 people. You'd use an early, deliberately narrow tool: you type
> things in yourself, it proposes one next step, and you accept, edit, defer, or dismiss it. Nothing
> is connected to your calendar or your messages unless you separately turn it on, and calendar
> sharing isn't even offered until the tool has been useful to you at least once.
>
> It is not a medical or therapeutic product and it makes no health claims.
>
> You can see everything it holds about you, switch it to quiet mode, revoke access, or delete your
> data at any time, and leaving the pilot doesn't lose your data.
>
> [PILOT TERMS — duration, time commitment, and any compensation. Owned by #55, not by #54.]
>
> Interested? Reply yes and I'll send the access details. Reply no and that's the end of it — it
> doesn't affect anything about the interview you already did.

## R8 — Declined the pilot, or withdrew

> Understood, and thank you — that's completely fine and it changes nothing about your interview.
>
> [If withdrawing from the study entirely:] I'll remove your interview from the study and delete the
> notes and the code linking them to you. I'll confirm when it's done, within a few days.

Set `pilot_status=declined`, or `withdrawn` with `withdrawn_at` and `deletion_completed` once the
deletion actually runs.

## R9 — Referral request, after an interview

Use sparingly. Referrals cluster: friends of a person with a problem tend to share the problem, which
inflates the pain rate. Track them as `source_channel=referral`, and keep referral plus
`personal_network` well under half the sample.

> One last thing, and please ignore it if you'd rather not: if you know one or two other people who
> deal with this regularly, I'd be glad of an introduction. I'll ask them the same screening
> questions and I won't mention that you referred them.

Never tell a referred candidate who referred them.
